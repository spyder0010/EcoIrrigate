# Combining measurement scale and sampling sensitivity

The current audit bounds the paired empirical MSE difference over a family of monotone scales on an unchanged forecast ledger. A stable sign over that family says nothing by itself about a new set of forecast origins. This diagnostic keeps the models, transformation knots and slope family fixed and asks how much the difference can vary when the paired forecast cases are resampled.

For a horizon and a collection of site slope vectors, the score difference is the sum of `s[k]ᵀ Q[k] s[k]`, where each `Q[k]` includes the site's share of the pooled origin count. A resample changes the weighted matrices by `D[k]`. Write `s[k] = 1 + u[k]`. The eight bins have equal widths, so the admissible offsets sum to zero and each lies in [−δ, δ]. Expand the quadratic around the identity map. The exact largest linear term in absolute value is twice δ times the difference between the sums of the largest and smallest four entries of `D[k] 1`. For the quadratic remainder, double centering `D[k]` leaves its quadratic form on zero-sum offsets unchanged. Its elementwise absolute sum times δ squared bounds that remainder. Use the absolute change at the identity plus the sum of these site bounds to bound the maximum deviation across all maps in each replicate. This avoids solving a separate optimization problem for every resample. The bound can be conservative.

Use the 95th percentile of these replicate bounds to enlarge the existing finite-sample outer interval in both directions. This reports an exploratory simultaneous **scale and resampling sensitivity envelope per horizon**. As a comparison, give the same replicate statistic at the identity map and re-evaluate the fixed common 24-hour reversal example from the original scale audit. Report 0.25 as the primary illustrative slope budget.

- EcoIrrigate uses 4,000 circular moving-block replicates at each of 48, 96 and 192 15-minute steps. Draw time blocks jointly for both farms, keep all forecasts for the five saved seeds paired, and use the same time indices at all horizons. Emphasize the 96-step result. The wrap is a resampling convention and can join the ends of the observed record. [Künsch (1989)](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176347265) gives the classic block-bootstrap context.
- The fixed USCRN Ridge comparison uses 10,000 whole-station resamples of the 35 eligible stations. Keep each sampled station's forecasts together and recompute the pooled case weights. This probes station composition in the existing cohort.

The resulting envelopes are descriptive. The original case selection was exploratory, the short EcoIrrigate interval contains few separate weather episodes, and the USCRN stations are a convenience cohort. We do not infer a network-wide or future-season coverage guarantee. [Yen and Yen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.02048) also use resampling for comparisons across a loss family. Here the indexed functions are common transformations of recorded predictions and outcomes, with a directly computable bound over a declared measurement scale family.

## Results

At δ = 0.25, the combined numerical ranges include zero at all four EcoIrrigate horizons with 96-step blocks and at all USCRN horizons under whole-station resampling. With 48-step EcoIrrigate blocks, the one-hour range remains positive. With 96- and 192-step blocks it includes zero. At 24 hours the fixed common reversal map's 96-step percentile interval is [−0.641, +0.438] squared percentage points. In 22.7% of those replicates, the identity score favors persistence while this fixed alternative scale favors BiLSTM. That fraction describes the chosen resampling exercise and is neither a significance test nor a probability that the physical calibration reverses the ranking.
